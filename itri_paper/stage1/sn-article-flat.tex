\abstract{\textbf{Purpose:} Large language model (LLM) assistants for pre-consultation must behave predictably and exchange data with electronic health records (EHRs). We present ELVINA (EHR-Linked Virtual Interviewer with Natural-language Assistance), a pre-consultation system built on FIRM (FHIR-Integrated Interviewing with Replaceable Modules), in which physician-authored questionnaires, not the LLM, select questions and results are written back as Taiwan Core (TW Core) FHIR resources, and we verify it against explicit requirements.

\textbf{Methods:} Questionnaires are encoded as FHIR Questionnaire. LLMs only extract evidence-grounded chief-complaint findings, classify EHR history into questionnaire options, and draft reports; one transaction writes QuestionnaireResponse, Observation, Composition, and Provenance. Ten requirements (R0--R9) were checked with synthetic Synthea patients and \ScenarioCount{} author-drafted scenarios answered by an LLM-simulated patient: interoperability, end-to-end runs, configuration-only model replacement, and local load. Clinician confirmation was simulated.

\textbf{Results:} Requirements \PassingRequirements{} passed and \FailingRequirements{} failed. Question selection (R0) was \RZeroVerdict{}, and structural/profile checks of \EOneResources{} written resources (R1, without terminology resolution) were \ROneVerdict{}. After EHR-history classification was added, written-FHIR and runtime question paths agreed in \ETwoWrittenPathAgree{} of \ETwoWrittenPathCompared{} comparable sessions (R3, \RThreeVerdict{}). Replacing the commercial model by configuration alone was \EThreeAlternateSwapVerdict{}; a local open-weight model was \EThreeLocalSwapVerdict{}. Red-flag rule coverage (R5) was \RFiveVerdict{}; candidate rules await physician approval. National-scale capacity was \REightVerdict{} and exchange-hub integration \RNineVerdict{}.

\textbf{Conclusions:} A rule-governed pre-consultation system with FHIR write-back can be verified requirement by requirement. These provisional, synthetic software-verification results do not establish clinical safety, diagnostic performance, or deployment readiness.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}

Low-acuity visits place a growing burden on unscheduled care worldwide.\cite{pinesInternationalPerspectivesEmergency2011} In Taiwan, despite low-cost access under National Health Insurance, its emergency departments remain chronically crowded, and about one-third of visits are non-emergent.\cite{huangCharacteristicsNonEmergentVisits2019} This crowding prolongs waits, worsens outcomes, and drives clinician burnout.\cite{morleyEmergencyDepartmentCrowding2018, ghaseminejadOrganisationalFactorsAssociated2025}

Large language models (LLMs) now support numerous clinical tasks, including ambient scribing, EHR documentation, and clinical decision support.\cite{nairCurrentStateDigital2026} Agentic systems extend these capabilities through planning, tool use, and multi-step action, enabling closer integration with health-system workflows and conversational diagnosis.\cite{tuConversationalDiagnosticArtificial2025} However, although various benchmarks now evaluate LLMs on realistic tasks and interactive encounters, most agentic systems remain confined to simulated settings.\cite{liuBenchmarkingLargeLanguage2026}

Here, we present ELVINA, an LLM-based agent for adults presenting in Taiwanese urgent care centers. ELVINA conducts structured history-taking by text or voice, drafts a clinician-ready note, and supports decision-making with a ranked differential and not-to-miss conditions, alongside suggested examinations and imaging. To limit hallucination, the LLM only extracts clinical facts linked to the patient's own words, while safety screening, question selection, and diagnostic ranking follow auditable rules. ELVINA connects to EHRs via SMART on FHIR and was evaluated on benchmark.



\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Scope and implemented architecture}
We verified ELVINA, FIRM's reference implementation, using synthetic
data. Outcomes concern interchange, interview-to-write-back completion, model
replacement, and local concurrency; clinical accuracy and note quality were outside scope.

ELVINA combines Vue patient and clinician interfaces, a FastAPI
backend, local retrieval, and a HAPI FHIR R4 sandbox (Fig.~\ref{fig:system})
\citep{vueJs,fastApi,hapiFHIR,fhirR4}.
Invitations yield scoped session cookies; the clinician launcher binds patient and
encounter identifiers. The separate SMART surface uses another authorization flow
\citep{mandelSmartOnFhir2016,smartAppLaunch}. Verification exercised text HTTP APIs,
using isolated databases and an explicit loopback development authorization bypass.

question and skip prefilled fields. Models classify prefill and extract chief

Keywords choose the questionnaire first; only no match permits evidence-grounded
model routing. Failed chief assessment triggers handoff. Retrieval supports report
drafts \citep{lewisRag2020}, without establishing diagnoses or orders. The alternative
engine making per-turn model calls was not evaluated.

\subsection{Conservative EHR prefill normalization}
UCC, launcher, and sandbox ingress deterministically number chronic-history items.
The configured report-extraction adapter returns only indices and fixed categories:
diabetes, chronic kidney disease, hyperlipidemia, cirrhosis, autoimmune disease, cancer,
other chronic, exclude, or unclear. Listed categories become questionnaire options;
other chronic retains original text. Any unclear item, provider failure, timeout,
or invalid/incomplete indices leaves both history fields unfilled for patient
answers. Otherwise, cancer/autoimmune/other chronic items also prefill
\texttt{chronic\_detail}; enabled but prefilled questions are skipped. No recognized
condition leaves \texttt{chronic} unfilled, without inferring ``none.'' Original
history, excluded items, classifications, model/prompt provenance, and bounded
usage remain available for physician review. Oversized inputs are explicitly
rejected; failures never echo history. Normalization sends history to the report
provider, defaults off, and was enabled for these runs.

\subsection{Questionnaire and write-back contracts}
The encoder maps fields to \texttt{linkId}s, options to codes, and conditions to
\texttt{enableWhen}. Questionnaire defines the form; QuestionnaireResponse records
answers \citep{fhirQuestionnaireR4,sdcIG}. Source JSON drives execution. Route
selection pins the source-digest canonical version; write-back rejects drift and
tags unpinned legacy resolution. R3 checks representation agreement.

After simulated clinician confirmation, opt-in structured write-back creates
Table~\ref{tab:fhir}'s transaction; Composition-only remains default. Conditional
creation supports retries. Patient/encounter/institution/issuer checks precede
persistence. Differential diagnoses, orders, and safety Flags are not created.

extensions. A runtime answer that FHIR would drop causes non-retryable rejection;
unanswered branches do not block writing.

\subsection{Synthetic inputs and model disclosure}
Synthea generated the adult source cohort \citep{syntheaMethod,syntheaSoftware}.
Conversion applies fictional Taiwanese identifiers/addresses and TW Core profiles
\citep{twcoreIG}, retaining seeds, mappings, dropped elements, and hashes. The
\ScenarioCount{} author-drafted chest-pain, headache, and abdominal-pain scenarios
include expected stops and known gaps; the planned benchmark was unavailable.
Separate Synthea modules supply consistent backgrounds without manually rewriting
generated records. Input validation is separate from output validation.

Background audits are mechanical; approval requires two independent physicians
reviewing current script hashes, bindings, and paths. \ValidatedScriptCount{}
scripts qualify. Online Resource 1 provides prompts/settings. Commercial calls
use Gemini's API \citep{googleGeminiAPI}; Gemma/Qwen use Ollama
\citep{ollamaSoftware,gemmaThree,qwenTwoFive}. Requested/transmitted settings are
distinguished: newer Gemini configurations omit temperature. Unrecorded settings
are not inferred.

\subsection{Verification activities}
R0 audits saved calls for prefill normalization, chief extraction (at most once per
session), report generation, or RAG translation, and requires identical repeated
paths per script. Static inspection identifies the pure next-question selector.
Prefill classification can change skip decisions; cross-model paths are descriptive.
Bounded, incomplete usage cannot establish a complete call history.

E1 uses the pinned HL7/TW Core validator and generated definitions
\citep{hl7Validator,twcoreIG}. R1 requires zero written-resource errors/exceptions
across templated and E2 cohorts. Checks use \texttt{-tx n/a}, without terminology
resolution. R2 requires substantive write/read-back identity after excluding
specified server metadata/identifiers. Missing outcomes cannot pass; supplemental
E1 is counted once (Online Resource 1, Sections S3 and S7).

E2 spans invitation through read-back. A distinct Qwen model plays the patient
from scenario facts, following simulated-patient evaluation
\citep{tuConversationalDiagnosticArtificial2025,johriCraftMd2025,schmidgallAgentClinic2024}.
Invalid constrained answers receive explicit unknowns, never expected answers.
R3 compares presented top-level questions with \texttt{enableWhen} paths derived
from written QuestionnaireResponse answers, including EHR prefill. Recorded
prefilled fields retain skip context for no-data items omitted from QR; nested
\texttt{<linkId>.other} details are not questions. Runtime-answer and script-derived
checks remain secondary. Expected chief stops are excluded; unexpected stops fail.
R4 requires every persisted chief fact to have traceable utterance evidence.
R5 requires all scripted red-flag and non-red-flag expectations, measuring rule
execution rather than clinical sensitivity/specificity. R6 requires write-back
and \texttt{completed} or \texttt{summary\_ready}; expected stops and successful
retries qualify, manual handoffs do not.

E3 retains executable fingerprints across model configurations. R7 requires zero
code changes and default-equivalent R1--R6 verdicts and per-script completion,
paths, and red flags; shared failures remain in the baseline. One-time adapter
changes against \texttt{\AdapterBaseCommit} are documented separately.

E4 replays turns using fixed-latency mocks or real models on backend v0.19.0,
one worker, stopping above \ELoadStopThreshold{} request errors. Session failure
is distinct; this local rule does not define national capacity. E5 needs MOHW
exchange-hub access. Frozen runs and source hashes generate results; repetitions
are not independent clinical observations.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Principal findings}
ELVINA keeps question selection under physician-authored rules and confines LLMs to
bounded tasks: chief-complaint extraction with verbatim evidence, classification
of EHR history into questionnaire options, and draft reports. The verification
shows that this division, which FIRM prescribes, can be checked requirement by
requirement. Question selection (R0) was \RZeroVerdict{}; written-resource
conformance and read-back (R1, R2) were \ROneVerdict{} and \RTwoVerdict{}; written
and runtime question paths (R3) were \RThreeVerdict{}; fact traceability (R4) and
end-to-end completion (R6) were \RFourVerdict{} and \RSixVerdict{}. Replacing the
commercial model through configuration alone was \EThreeAlternateSwapVerdict{},
supporting the claim that model-dependent components sit behind a replaceable
adapter.

LLM-led diagnostic dialogue systems select their own questions
\citep{tuConversationalDiagnosticArtificial2025}, and evaluations that require
models to elicit information through conversation report weaker performance than
vignette-based testing \citep{johriCraftMd2025,schmidgallAgentClinic2024}. ELVINA
trades conversational flexibility for predictable, auditable question selection;
that predictability is what made path-level verification possible. EHR-history
classification does influence which history questions are skipped, but the next
question is still chosen by the questionnaire rules. Whether fixed questionnaires
elicit as much relevant history as free dialogue is a separate empirical question
not addressed here.

\subsection{Defects found by verification}
The checks exposed defects that component tests had not revealed. First, the
runtime evaluated free-text EHR history by substring while the written FHIR
representation used coded answers, so an earlier verification round found written
paths that disagreed with the questions actually asked. The remediation classifies
EHR history entries into questionnaire options by index and leaves both history
questions to the patient whenever any entry is unclear or classification fails;
in the selected runs R3 is \RThreeVerdict{} against the unchanged written-resource
criterion. Second, prefilled history was silently truncated at a fixed length;
oversized input is now rejected explicitly. Third, chief-complaint findings for
suspected pulmonary embolism and for abdominal pain radiating to the back with a
known aneurysm were extracted correctly, but no rule combined them (R5,
\RFiveVerdict{}: \RFiveFailingScripts{}). Candidate rules drafted from current
guidance \citep{konstantinidesEscPe2020,wanhainenEsvsAaa2024} await physician
approval and new physician-written scenarios, and were not enabled. Finally,
screening runs only at the chief complaint, so later questionnaire selections such
as fainting do not raise an alert; simulated-patient answers that contradicted
their scripts illustrated this gap.

Configuration-level replaceability did not guarantee behavioural equivalence. The
local open-weight model was \EThreeLocalSwapVerdict{} for R7 (\RSevenFailingScripts{}),
through chief-stage handoffs after timeouts and missed expected stops; its R3
failures also reflect those handoffs rather than path disagreement. Each model
replacement therefore needs re-verification against the same requirements.
National capacity (R8) needs an authoritative demand target, and exchange-hub
interoperability (R9) needs authorized test access.

\subsection{Limitations and clinical review needs}
Verdicts remain provisional pending physician review of the scenarios and
independent audits. Requirement-defined criteria were applied post~hoc, and
clinician confirmation was simulated. The LLM-simulated patient sometimes
contradicted its script; these answers were recorded as patient-reported and limit
scenario fidelity rather than the path and write-back checks. EHR-history
classification is not clinical confirmation: the default model assigned some
entries to listed options that a provisional reference does not support, such as
prediabetes as diabetes and colon polyps as cancer
(\EOnePrefillListedDisagree{} entries in the templated cohort), excluded some
chronic conditions, and was not fully consistent across sessions (Online
Resource~1, Section S6). These answers are written back as unconfirmed EHR prefill,
and the original EHR text remains available to clinicians; the classifier was not
modified for this study. Its reference labels lack physician review, and synthetic
disease-name distributions do not establish accuracy on real EHR data.
Structural/profile checks ran without terminology resolution. Synthetic
demographics and sandbox interoperability do not establish clinical utility,
production authorization, tenant isolation, browser SMART integration, or
deployment readiness; terminology review, institutional governance, and human
approval remain necessary.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
ELVINA, built on the FIRM framework, keeps question selection under
physician-authored rules and writes results back as TW Core FHIR resources, and it
can be verified against explicit requirements. Requirements \PassingRequirements{}
passed and \FailingRequirements{} failed in this software verification; the failures
identify concrete defects with documented remediations or pending clinical
decisions. Replacing the commercial model by configuration was
\EThreeAlternateSwapVerdict{}, whereas a local open-weight model was
\EThreeLocalSwapVerdict{}. Clinical validation, national-scale capacity
(\REightVerdict{}), and exchange-hub integration (\RNineVerdict{}) remain future work.
