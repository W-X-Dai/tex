\section{Summary}
% Summarize in your own words the paper, its key ideas, its contributions and their significance. This summary helps the AC and the authors understand the rest of your review and be confident that you understand the paper.
This paper proposes SwAV (Swapping Assignments between Views), an online clustering-based self-supervised method for visual representation learning. 
Instead of comparing features of different views directly as in contrastive learning, it assigns each view to learnable prototypes and predicts the cluster assignment of one view from the feature of another view of the same image. 
Together with multi-crop, a data augmentation strategy that adds low-resolution crops at little extra cost, SwAV achieves 75.3\% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet linear evaluation with ResNet-50 and surpasses supervised pretraining on all considered transfer tasks.

\section{Strengths}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Memory efficient and flexible in batch size. SwAV avoids pairwise feature comparisons and requires neither a large memory bank nor a momentum encoder. It stores only about 3.8K features and works well with both large (4096) and small (256) batches, making it usable on GPUs with small VRAM.
    \item Effective multi-crop augmentation. Adding several low-resolution crops to the two standard views increases the number of views at little extra memory cost, and consistently improves SimCLR, DeepCluster, and SeLa by 2-4\% top-1 accuracy.
    \item Truly online method. Unlike DeepCluster and SeLa, SwAV computes codes within each mini-batch. This allows it to scale to unlimited data, as shown by pretraining on 1B uncurated Instagram images.
    \item Comprehensive experiments. SwAV is evaluated on ImageNet and multiple downstream tasks, surpassing supervised pretraining on all of them, with a fair ablation against contrastive methods.
\end{enumerate}
